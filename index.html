About me

I work in extremal combinatorics and graph theory. Before joining Georgia Tech, I was a research fellow at the National University of Singapore, where I worked with Hao Huang. I received my Ph.D. in mathematics from the University of Science and Technology of China, advised by Jie Ma, and also completed my undergraduate degree there.

Research

I study how local restrictions determine the size and structure of graphs, hypergraphs, and set families. Some questions I have worked on include:

  • Cycles in graphs. How many edges can a graph have without a 4-cycle? What structure must a near-extremal graph have, and how many 4-cycles appear once the edge threshold is exceeded? Related paper
  • Hypergraph Turán problems. How large can a uniform hypergraph be if a specified configuration is forbidden? I have studied generalized 4-cycles and how the parity of the uniformity changes bipartite Turán problems. Related preprint
  • Coloring and critical graphs. How many colors are needed to partition the edges of a bipartite graph into induced matchings? Another direction is counting edges and critical subgraphs in graphs that are minimal with respect to their chromatic number. Related preprint
  • Extremal set theory. How large can a uniform set family be if it cannot realize every trace on a set of a given size? Related preprint
